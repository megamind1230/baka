*** 📚 Section 1: What Is A Blockchain?
This section establishes the foundational knowledge by exploring the
"why" and "how" of blockchain technology.
- *Core Problem & Solution:* It begins by examining the problems with
  centralized systems (like censorship or unverifiable processes) and
  presents blockchain as a decentralized, shared ledger where truth is
  verified by math, not intermediaries .
- *History & Evolution:* You'll trace the technology's origins, from the
  double-spend problem that plagued early digital cash to Bitcoin's
  revolutionary solution, and then to Ethereum's evolution into a
  platform for smart contracts and "world computer" .
- *Key Benefits:* The section covers the main advantages of blockchain,
  including its permissionless, immutable, and credibly neutral
  properties .
- *Initial Use Cases:* It provides a practical overview of blockchain's
  current impact, including DeFi, cross-border payments, and DAOs .
- *Multi-Chain World:* It demystifies the landscape of Layer 1 (L1),
  Layer 2 (L2) blockchains, and explains the crucial difference between
  real-money mainnets and risk-free testnets .
- *The Oracle Problem:* A crucial lesson on why smart contracts cannot
  access off-chain data on their own, and how Decentralized Oracle
  Networks (DONs) act as a secure bridge to the real world .
*** ⚙️ Section 2: Smart Contracts & The EVM
This module dives deep into the "programs" that run on the blockchain.
- *What is a Smart Contract:* You'll learn how these are "math-based
  promises" or "unbreakable digital agreements" that offer advantages
  over traditional, trust-based contracts .
- *Benefits of Smart Contracts:* This part explores their revolutionary
  properties: decentralization, immutability, and transparency, which
  replace fragile trust with cryptographic certainty .
- *Introduction to the EVM:* You'll get a primer on the Ethereum Virtual
  Machine (EVM), the core computation engine of Ethereum, and learn the
  difference between EVM Equivalence and Compatibility .
*** 💸 Section 3: Sending Transactions (Hands-On)
This is where the course gets practical, guiding you through real-world
interactions with the blockchain using testnets.
- *Setting Up a Wallet:* A detailed, step-by-step guide to creating a
  secure MetaMask wallet, covering the critical concept of self-custody
  and the importance of your Secret Recovery Phrase .
- *Testnets & Tenderly:* You'll learn about the friction of public
  testnet faucets and how Cyfrin's use of Tenderly virtual testnets
  removes this pain point, allowing you to spin up a private blockchain
  with infinite test ETH .
- *Your First Transaction:* A guided walkthrough to send your first ETH
  transaction on a virtual testnet, and then on a public Sepolia
  testnet, where you can use Etherscan to track your activity .
*** ⛽ Section 4: Gas, Wallets & Network Mechanics
This section breaks down the technical mechanics behind blockchain
operations.
- *Transaction Signing:* An in-depth explanation of the process of
  signing transactions, including the crucial role of public and private
  keys for security .
- *What is Gas:* You will learn why every transaction requires a fee,
  breaking down "Gas" as computational effort and "Gas Price" as a bid
  for priority. It also covers calculating total costs and Ethereum's
  EIP-1559 fee structure .
- *How Blockchains Work:* A detailed look at the inner workings,
  including hash functions and the concept of immutability .
- *Scaling with L2s:* An overview of Layer 1s, Layer 2s, and rollups
  (Optimistic and ZK), explaining why they are essential for solving
  blockchain's scalability challenges .
*** 🚀 Section 5: Real-World Blockchain Use Cases
This is designed to be a more relaxed and conceptual section, focusing
on the practical applications of the technology .
- *Overview:* It connects the dots between the complex architecture
  you've just learned and the tangible, real-world impact of Web3 .
- *DeFi (Decentralized Finance):* Explores how developers are building
  an alternative, open financial system on the blockchain, including
  lending and decentralized exchanges (DEXs) .
- *Tokens:* A breakdown of different token standards, including fungible
  (ERC20) and non-fungible (NFT) tokens .
- *CEXs vs. DEXs:* A comparison of centralized exchanges (like Coinbase)
  with DEXs (like Uniswap) .
- *Stablecoins:* An examination of how cryptocurrencies are designed to
  maintain a stable value, often pegged to a real-world currency .
- *Real World Assets (RWA):* Explores the tokenization of physical
  assets like real estate on the blockchain .
- *DAOs:* An introduction to Decentralized Autonomous Organizations, or
  internet-native, community-led organizations .
*** 🔐 Section 6: Blockchain Threats and Security
This section highlights the risks and attack vectors in the blockchain
space.
- *Attack Vectors:* You will learn about critical threats like
  Miner/Maximal Extractable Value (MEV), 51% attacks, Sybil attacks, and
  blockchain reorganizations .
- *Advanced Concepts:* The course also introduces topics like account
  abstraction (EIP-4337) and concludes with a celebratory summary of
  your learning journey from theory to practice .
