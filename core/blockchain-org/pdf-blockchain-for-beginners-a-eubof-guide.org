**** isn't blockchain all about decentralization, how can CBDC be an application of blockchain then?
*Excellent question.*
The important distinction is:
#+begin_quote
*Blockchain does not automatically mean fully decentralized.*
#+end_quote
Blockchain is fundamentally a way of maintaining a *shared,
tamper-resistant ledger using a network of computers*.
There are different ways to organize that network.
For example:
#+begin_src text
             BLOCKCHAIN
                  │
       ┌──────────┴──────────┐
       ↓                     ↓
  Decentralized          Permissioned
     public                 network
       │                     │
 Bitcoin/Ethereum       Banks/government
#+end_src
A *CBDC* (Central Bank Digital Currency) is digital money issued by a
country's *central bank*.
A central bank could potentially use blockchain or distributed-ledger
technology to operate some part of a CBDC system.
But it *doesn't need to be decentralized like Bitcoin*.
For example:
#+begin_src text
  Bitcoin
     ↓
  No central authority
     ↓
  Highly decentralized
  CBDC
     ↓
  Central bank remains responsible
     ↓
  Can use a permissioned blockchain
#+end_src
So there's no contradiction.
*Blockchain is a technology. Decentralization is a design choice.*

**** Page 8
- *Why Blockchain Matters (Key Advantages):*
  1. *Decentralization:* No single point of failure.
  2. *Transparency & Immutability:* Data is very difficult to alter,
     creating a trustworthy ledger.
  3. *Peer-to-Peer (p2p) Transactions*
  4. *Censorship Resistance & Public Verification:* Open networks allow
     for transparency and prevent unilateral control.
**** Page 9
- *The Case of Bitcoin:*
  - *Bitcoin vs Blockchain:* Bitcoin is the first and most famous
    /application/ of blockchain, not the technology itself.
  - *Mining:* The "trust system" in Bitcoin relies on "mining," which
    uses computing power to confirm transactions and *generate new
    bitcoins*.
**** Page 10:12 *Fundamentals:*
- *Decentralization:*
  - *Distributed Ledger:* The ledger is spread across multiple "nodes"
    instead of one central point.
  - *Consensus Mechanism:* Transactions are agreed upon by a network
    consensus rather than a central authority.
  - *Security and Trust:* Uses cryptography for security, and trust is
      established through the network's collective agreement.
- *Transparency and Immutability:* Transactions are public and cannot
  be altered once added.
- *Cryptography:*
  - *Digital Signatures:* Users sign transactions with a private key,
      and others verify them with the public key
  - *Hash Functions:* Acts like a digital fingerprint for data, securing
      the link between blocks and maintaining the integrity of the
      blockchain.
- *Consensus Mechanisms:* a set of rules or protocols for the network to agree on the validity of the ledger.
  - *Types of Consensus:*
    - *Proof of Work (PoW):* Used by Bitcoin, secure but =energy-intensive=.
    - *Proof of Stake (PoS):* =less energy=, validators are chosen based on the amount of =cryptocurrency they "stake."=
    - *Delegated Proof of Stake (dPoS):* A =democratic= version where stakeholders vote for delegates.
    - *Proof of Authority (PoA):* Transactions are validated by =approved, trusted accounts=; faster but less decentralized.
**** Page 14
- *Blockchain vs Traditional Databases (continued):*
  - *When to Use Blockchain:*
    - When immutable and tamper-proof records are needed.
    - decentralization
    - transparency and auditability.
    - using smart contracts.
    - strong cryptographic security.
    - collaborative trust across organizations.
    - managing cryptocurrencies or tokens.
    - applications requiring a consensus.
- *limitations of blockchain:* Blockchain may not be suitable for applications
  requiring high-speed data processing or low latency. Sometimes a
  hybrid approach is best.
**** Page 15-16 *Types of Blockchain:*
- *Public vs Private Blockchain:*
  - *Public:* Open to anyone, decentralized, and permissionless (e.g.,
    Bitcoin, Ethereum).
  - *Private:* Requires permission to join, provides more control and
    privacy for businesses.
- *Permissionless vs Permissioned Blockchain:*
  - *Permissionless:* No gatekeepers, anyone can join and transact
    publicly.
  - *Permissioned:* Only identifiable participants can join
- *Consortium Blockchain:* Used by a group of organizations to enable
  *private* transactions among trusted participants (e.g., R3 Corda,
  Quorum).
- *Hybrid Blockchain:* *both private and public*

**** Page 17:25 *Blockchain Use Cases:*
1. *Finance and Cryptocurrency:*
   - *Finance:* The broad field of managing money, investments, and
       financial resources.
   - *Cryptocurrency:* A digital/virtual currency that uses
       cryptography for security, operates on a decentralized blockchain,
       and is not controlled by any central authority.
2. *Supply Chain Management:*
   - *Enhancing Transparency, Traceability, and Security:* immutable
     ledger for recording every transaction in the supply chain.
   - *Authenticity Verification:* Products can be assigned unique
     digital IDs to prove their authenticity.
   - *Smart Contracts:* Can automate processes like payment and quality
     control.
   - Other benefits include optimizing inventory, ensuring quality and
     compliance, and tracking environmental impact.
3. *Healthcare:*
   - *Electronic Health Records (EHRs):* Blockchain provides a secure and
     interoperable way to store and share patient data.
   - *Medical Data Sharing*
   - *Supply Chain Management:* Ensures drug authenticity and reduces
     counterfeiting.
   - *Patient Consent Management:* Patients can control access to their
     data via smart contracts.
   - *Other Uses:* Telemedicine, healthcare provider credential
     verification, insurance claims processing, and securing
     patient-managed health records.
4. *Voting and Governance:*
   - *Secure Voting Systems*
   - *Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs):* Self-governing
     entities that use smart contracts for decision-making.
   - *Other Applications:* Transparent government records, token-based
     voting, and decentralized decision-making.
5. *Intellectual Property:*
   - *Ownership Verification:* Blockchain can establish and verify
     =ownership and origin of IP assets.=
   - *Timestamps:* Creates indisputable proof of creation date for
     copyright purposes.
   - *Smart Contracts:* Automates royalty payments and licensing
     agreements.
   - *Counterfeit Prevention:* Creates digital certificates of
     authenticity for physical products.
6. *NFTs (Non-Fungible Tokens):* A unique and non-interchangeable
   digital asset verified on a blockchain.  Evolved from "colored
   coins" on Bitcoin into projects like =CryptoPunks and CryptoKitties=.
   - *Key Characteristics:* Uniqueness, rarity (artificial, numerical,
     historical), proof of ownership, immutability, and
     programmability.
7. *DeFi (Decentralized Finance):*
   - *Traditional Finance (TradFi):* Centralized, custodial,
     permissioned, costly, and can be exclusive.
   - *DeFi:* A paradigm shift to a decentralized, open, and
     permissionless peer-to-peer financial system.
   - *Key Differences:* DeFi uses triple-entry accounting, is mostly
     trustless, has public transaction data, is highly innovative, can be
     difficult to use, and has irreversible transactions.
   - *Risks:* DeFi carries inherent technology (dev framework), project, economic, and
     governance risks.
8. *Virtual Worlds (Metaverse):*
   - *Definition:* Persistent, immersive environments that blend physical
     and digital worlds in real-time.
   - *Closed vs Open Virtual Worlds:*
     - *Closed:* Controlled by a few powerful entities (e.g., tech
       giants). Decisions are based on shareholder value, and user data
       is stored centrally.
     - *Open:* Decentralized and community-based. Values are
       stakeholder-centric, and users have more control over their data.
9. *CBDCs (Central Bank Digital Currencies):*
   - *Current Money Forms:* Physical (coins/banknotes) and electronic
     (commercial bank accounts).
   - *Definition:* A CBDC is a form of digital money issued by a central
     bank.
   - *CBDC vs Cryptocurrency:* CBDCs are centralized, unlike
     decentralized cryptocurrencies.
  - *Two Models:*
    - *Wholesale:* Used by commercial banks and institutions for
      payments and settlements.
    - *Retail:* A form of legal tender for the public (individuals and
      businesses).
  - *Criticism and Concerns on CBDC:* Risks include financial instability,
    privacy issues, cyberattacks, marginalization of traditional
    banking, and potential for increased inequality.
**** Page 26
- *Interoperability:*
  - *Definition:* The ability of different blockchain networks to
    communicate, send messages, and exchange data and tokens.
  - *Importance:* It's vital for linking the currently fragmented
    blockchain ecosystems and preventing a new form of centralization.
  - *Challenges:* Includes ensuring transaction finality across chains,
    managing cross-chain smart contracts, and maintaining data
    integrity.
- *Environmental Concerns of blockchain and Solutions:*
  - *Problems:* Proof-of-Work (PoW) blockchains are highly
    energy-intensive, have a significant carbon footprint, and
    generate electronic waste.
  - *Solutions:*
    - *Alternative Consensus:* More energy-efficient mechanisms like PoS
      and PoA, as mentioned before.
    - *using Renewable Energy:* solar, wind, or hydroelectric power for
      mining.
    - *Optimization:* Making smart contracts more efficient.
    - *Hardware:* Using more efficient mining equipment.
    - *Carbon Offsets:* Investing in projects that reduce greenhouse
      gases.
